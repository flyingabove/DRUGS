# Marc Malone — "The Cancer Code" (Matt Beall Podcast)

Research notes compiled 2026-08-22. Source: web research into Marc Malone's claims as presented on the *Matt Beall Podcast* episode "The Cancer Code, Gene Reprogramming, & Searching for a Cure" (~July 2026), plus his company Airmid Biogenetics and associated publications.

## Episode context

Malone appeared on the *Matt Beall Podcast* discussing cancer biology, gene reprogramming, cancer stem cells, metabolism, and treatment resistance. He is not a credentialed scientist or clinician — he describes himself as self-taught, with no prior lab or oncology background, who began studying molecular biology after his wife was diagnosed with treatment-resistant triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). He is now the founder of **Airmid Biogenetics**.

## The scientific claims made

His framework, which he calls **"Triune Restoration,"** is built around three targets:

1. **Epigenetic gene silencing** — reactivating tumor-suppressor and hormone-receptor genes he says were epigenetically silenced (not mutated), including:
   - **ESR1** (estrogen receptor) and **PGR** (progesterone receptor) across chromosomes 6 and 11
   - **RARB** (retinoic acid receptor beta) restoration
2. **Cancer stem cell (CSC) pathway suppression** — targeting **Notch**, **WNT**, and **Hedgehog-GLI2** signaling, which he claims to have fully eliminated
3. **Metabolic/signaling modulation** — glycolysis/glutaminolysis shifts and "decoupling" the PI3K-mTOR axis

Additional claimed mechanisms/outcomes:
- Drops in **TP53, KMT2C, and PMS2** mutational burden (TP53 mutant allele burden reportedly halved within two weeks)
- Reduced circulating tumor cells (CTCs) and Ki-67
- Release of a **SIN3-HDAC** chromatin repressor complex
- Delivery via "re-engineered analog formulations of existing FDA-approved compounds"

**Reported outcome (his wife's case):** ~70% tumor reduction, survival extended from a 3.8-month prognosis to 12 months, described as achieved with zero toxicity.

He separately discusses a similar approach applied to his son, who he says carries 300+ functional mutations and has profound autism with cognitive disability / metabolic-neurodegeneration.

## The evidence behind it

- A single self-reported case study/preprint titled **"Functional Gene Restoration and Cancer Stem Cell Eradication in a Treatment-Resistant TNBC Case: A First-in-Human Paradigm Shift,"** posted to ResearchGate (also referenced on Cureus and Academia.edu profiles). No confirmed indexed journal name, formal peer-review record, or PubMed listing was found independently.
- Verification is described as third-party lab testing (**Tempus Labs**, **RGCC**) and review by "several dozen PhDs and MDs," plus two named reviewers (Dr. Erin Greer, MD; Zizu Ziblim, PhD Biochemistry) — but no raw data, assay methodology, or quantitative before/after values are publicly posted.
- One cited "validation" source is **ScholarAI**, an AI tool — not a scientific body or journal.

## Red flags / caveats

- **N=1, self-administered, self-reported.** A single case (his wife) is the entire evidentiary base; no independent replication exists.
- **No confirmed formal peer-reviewed publication.** The paper appears self-posted to ResearchGate/Academia.edu. Independent write-ups explicitly note "the article does not fully delineate the molecular pathways" and that findings need "urgent scientific replication."
- **Promotional media coverage.** Coverage in Grit Daily and Benzinga is sponsored/partner content with heavy marketing language (e.g., "ten Nobel-level breakthroughs") and no disclosed conflicts of interest.
- **No disclosed credentials.** No formal academic or clinical credentials for Malone are disclosed in any of these sources.

## Bottom line

The podcast presents a compelling personal narrative using real molecular-biology vocabulary (epigenetic silencing, CSC signaling pathways, specific gene names), but the actual scientific support is a single unreplicated, non-independently-verified case report from the person who both administered the treatment and authored the write-up — not established or peer-confirmed oncology research at this point.
